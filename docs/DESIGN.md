# Design: Remote Job Application Platform ("eligible jobs, applied fast")

## Context
The founder wants a platform that helps people find jobs abroad and applies for them (finds openings,
tailors and sends the CV, fills in the application form). The platform's job **ends at submission**.
After that, the candidate answers recruiters and handles the rest themselves. This doc commits to
**Approach B (full web platform)**, chosen by the founder.

## Problem Statement
Candidates outside the US/EU who want remote (or relocation-sponsored) jobs waste most of their time on
postings they can never be hired for ("Remote — US only", "EU work authorization required"). The jobs
that *are* open to them still need a tailored CV and the same data retyped into every ATS form
(ATS = the applicant tracking system a company uses to receive applications).

## Demand Evidence
Weak so far, and stated honestly: the founder's own pain/hunch. Nobody has yet been observed paying
for this or spending heavy hours on it. **This is the main risk, and the rollout plan below handles it
with a concierge phase.**

## Status Quo
Founder: finding real openings, adapting CVs and filling forms are "all three, equally" painful.
Today candidates use LinkedIn and remote boards (WeWorkRemotely, RemoteOK, Remotive) and filter by
eye; auto-apply bots (LazyApply, AIApply, LoopCV) spray volume, get ATS-blocked and have low reply
rates (Sonara shut down).

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
- Signups are open to anyone. v1 is **tuned** for candidates outside the US/EU seeking remote jobs
  truly open to their country, or with relocation/visa sponsorship.
- Wedge: **eligibility accuracy**. Each job carries "Open to <your country>: YES / NO / UNCLEAR", the
  supporting quote, and the hiring route (direct, EOR, contractor, sponsorship). EOR = employer of
  record, a service that legally employs someone on a company's behalf in another country.

## Premises (agreed)
1. Open signup; v1 is tuned for non-US/EU candidates seeking remote or relocation-sponsored jobs.
2. The platform's job ENDS at submission. It never replies to recruiters, schedules interviews or
   speaks for the candidate. Every form uses the **candidate's own email and phone**, so employer
   replies go straight to them and the platform can't sit in the middle.
3. No blind mass-apply. The platform prepares each application and the candidate clicks Approve.
4. (Revised after the second opinion) **v1 prepares** the application: tailored CV + answer pack +
   direct apply link. The candidate submits on the company's form. **v1.5 adds one-click auto-submit**
   for Greenhouse and Lever hosted forms, gated on concierge data (≥3 of 10 users ask for it).
   LinkedIn Easy Apply and Workday are permanently out of scope (terms of service, account per employer).
5. The platform logs what it prepared/sent (job, company, date, CV version). Replies aren't tracked
   (see Open Q1 on an optional day-14 "got a reply?" ping).
6. A concierge test with 5-10 real people runs alongside the build, before any spend on growth.

## Cross-Model Perspective (Claude subagent, cold read)
- Steelman: compete on "jobs that are truly open to you", not on volume.
- Critical: "all three, equally" hides the real pain; the founder's own insight points to eligibility.
- High: auto-submit in v1 is the most fragile and least differentiated part, which led to premise #4 being phased.
- Medium: no reply tracking means no proof the filter works, so add an opt-in day-14 ping.
- Suggested a 48h Telegram-bot prototype (the founder picked the full web platform instead).

## Approaches Considered
- **A: Telegram eligibility bot.** Rejected by the founder in favor of the full product. Its job-ingestion and classifier pieces are reused below.
- **C: Browser extension lens + autofill.** Not chosen for v1. A candidate path for v2 "fill on any site".

## Recommended Approach: B, Full Web Platform

### User flow
1. **Onboard:** sign up (email magic link or Google). Upload a CV (PDF/DOCX), parsed into a structured
   profile the candidate reviews and corrects: country of residence, citizenship(s), timezone, roles,
   seniority, salary floor, languages, work-authorization facts.
2. **Feed:** the candidate sees only jobs classified YES (UNCLEAR behind a toggle). Each card shows the
   eligibility quote, hiring route, salary if listed, and posted date.
3. **Prepare:** the candidate clicks Prepare. The platform generates a tailored CV (PDF) and an answer
   pack for the form's questions, using **only facts from the profile** (no invented experience,
   enforced in the prompt and by a diff check against the profile).
4. **Approve:** the candidate reviews and edits the CV and answers, then clicks Approve.
5. **Submit:** v1 opens the company's form, with a copy button for each answer and the CV ready to
   attach. In v1.5, approved jobs on Greenhouse or Lever submit automatically through a worker.
6. **Log:** the application moves to "Applied" with a timestamp and a snapshot of the CV. The
   platform's involvement ends here and the UI says so ("Replies go to your inbox. You take it from here.").

### Architecture
- **Web:** Next.js (App Router, TypeScript) + Tailwind; deploy on Vercel.
- **DB:** Postgres (Neon or Supabase) with Drizzle ORM. CV files go in S3-compatible storage (R2/Supabase
  Storage), encrypted at rest.
- **Job ingestion worker** (cron, every 6h): pulls from the **public job-board APIs** of Greenhouse
  (`boards-api.greenhouse.io/v1/boards/{token}/jobs?content=true`), Lever
  (`api.lever.co/v0/postings/{company}?mode=json`) and Ashby (`api.ashbyhq.com/posting-api/job-board/{org}`)
  for a seeded list of ~2,000 remote-friendly companies. No scraping; jobs are de-duplicated and
  removed when they disappear from the source.
- **Eligibility classifier:** Claude API (`claude-haiku-5-5` for volume, escalating UNCLEAR cases to
  `claude-sonnet-5-5`). Structured output: `{eligible_countries[], excluded_regions[], verdict_by_country,
  quote, hiring_route, confidence}`. Classified once per job (not per user), cached and re-run only
  when the posting changes.
- **Tailoring service:** Claude generates the CV and answers from profile + job; PDF via a headless
  HTML→PDF renderer (Playwright `page.pdf()` or React-PDF).
- **v1.5 submit worker:** a Playwright queue for Greenhouse/Lever *hosted* application forms only,
  run strictly after candidate approval, rate-limited, with a screenshot of the confirmation saved
  to the log. Any CAPTCHA or unknown field falls back to manual submission (never bypassed).
- **Jobs/queues:** a lightweight Postgres-backed queue (pg-boss) or Inngest.

### Data model (core tables)
`users`, `profiles` (structured CV facts, work-auth facts), `cv_files`, `companies` (ats_type,
board_token), `jobs` (source, raw, status), `job_eligibility` (job_id, country, verdict, quote, route,
model, confidence), `applications` (user_id, job_id, status: prepared|approved|submitted|failed,
cv_snapshot_id, answers_json, submitted_at, method: manual|auto), `classifier_labels` (human gold set).

### Trust, privacy, boundaries
- CVs are personal data: GDPR-style consent, export, and one-click full deletion; minimal retention.
- The platform never creates employer-facing accounts or inboxes for the candidate and never sends email to employers.
- A wrong YES is the worst bug. The gold set and precision bar below guard it.

## Rollout
1. **Week 1-2:** ingestion + classifier + gold set (hand-label 200 postings) → hit the precision bar.
2. **Week 2-3:** profile/CV parsing, feed, Prepare/Approve, Applied log (v1).
3. **Week 3-6 (concierge, in parallel):** onboard 10 named real candidates by hand, sit with them
   while they apply, time each step, and record who asks for auto-submit.
4. **v1.5:** Greenhouse/Lever auto-submit only if ≥3/10 concierge users ask for it.

## Success Criteria
- Classifier: ≥90% precision on YES and ≥70% recall, over 200 hand-labelled postings for the target country.
- Supply: ≥20 YES jobs/week in the candidate's role family (otherwise widen the region or add EOR roles).
- Concierge: ≥7/10 candidates still using it weekly after 3 weeks; median prepare+submit ≤10 min/application.
- Willingness to pay: ≥3/10 say yes to a concrete price (e.g. $9-15/month) *and* enter payment details.

## Open Questions
1. Opt-in day-14 "got a reply?" ping: in or out? (Recommended in; it never contacts employers.)
2. Monetization: subscription vs pay-per-prepared-application vs freemium (N free/month).
3. Which country does the founder launch tuning for? (Likely Kazakhstan/Central Asia, unconfirmed.)
4. Seed company list source for the ~2,000 remote-friendly companies on Greenhouse/Lever/Ashby.

## Distribution Plan
A web service on Vercel; GitHub Actions for CI (typecheck, unit tests, classifier eval against the gold
set on every prompt change); auto-deploy on merge to main.

## Dependencies
Anthropic API key; Postgres + object storage; domain; the hand-labelled gold set (founder time).

## Verification (when built)
- `classifier eval` script: run the model over the 200-posting gold set, print precision/recall per verdict; CI fails below the bar.
- E2E (Playwright): sign up → upload sample CV → feed shows only YES jobs → Prepare → Approve → Applied log entry.
- Ingestion test: fixture responses from the three board APIs → correct de-duplication and closure handling.
- v1.5: auto-submit against a Greenhouse sandbox/test board only; never hit live employers in tests.

## The Assignment
Before writing code: **write down 10 named people outside the US/EU who are looking for a remote job
right now, and sit with 2 of them (screen-share is fine) while they apply to one job, without helping.**
Time each step: finding, checking eligibility, tailoring, form-filling. That timing settles whether
the real pain is "all three, equally" or one step, and it gives you your first concierge users.

## What I noticed about how you think
- You drew the boundary yourself, up front: "this platform should serve only when applying for the job,
  and later user is responsible." That one line removes most of the legal and trust risk in this space.
- When asked for one specific person, you answered "anyone can apply". Under pressure you picked
  "Global → remote jobs", which removed the visa problem entirely. Your instincts narrow well once forced.
- You chose the full platform over my recommended bot. That's conviction, and it's fine. Just let the
  concierge numbers above be what can change your mind, not momentum.
